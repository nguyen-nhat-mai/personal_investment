Where the data comes from

French real estate — DVF

"Demandes de Valeurs Foncières" — essentially every property sale in France, published as open data by the French government (data.gouv.fr). Refreshed roughly twice a year. Doesn't cover Alsace-Moselle (57/67/68), which uses a different land registry, or most overseas départements.

Commune demographics & income — INSEE

Population, area, density, and median household disposable income per commune, from two INSEE-sourced data.gouv.fr datasets. Reference data, refreshed roughly yearly.

Equities — Yahoo Finance

Daily prices (adjusted for dividends/splits), dividends, and splits for the PEA watchlist: CAC 40 constituents, a handful of non-French EU blue chips also PEA-eligible under the EU/EEA-headquarters rule (e.g. ASML, SAP), and PEA-eligible ETFs (world/US/emerging-markets/Europe/small-cap trackers), pulled via yfinance on weekday evenings. Legal PEA eligibility (this list) and whether your specific broker's PEA custody actually supports a given foreign-listed line aren't always the same thing — verify with your broker.

Property tax rates — DGFiP

Commune-level building property tax rates ("taxe foncière sur les propriétés bâties"), from the French tax authority's "Fiscalité locale des particuliers" dataset (data.gouv.fr / data.economie.gouv.fr). Reference data, refreshed yearly. Shown in the Real estate table, not yet part of the opportunity score.

Population history — "Populations légales" 2017-2021

Year-by-year official population per commune, feeding the population-growth factor in the score. A community republish (data.gouv.fr org "icem7") of INSEE figures rather than first-party INSEE hosting — the official INSEE bulk API proved too slow/unreliable to fetch reliably from this environment, this source is hosted on the same fast infrastructure DVF uses. Static reference data (2017–2021 is the fullest span available as of this writing, not automatically current).

How it's processed

Each source has its own Airflow pipeline that downloads, lightly cleans, and lands raw data in BigQuery. dbt then transforms it through three layers — staging (1:1 cleanup per source), intermediate (aggregations, joins, YoY math), and marts (the business-facing tables this dashboard reads) — with automated tests checking things like uniqueness and non-null keys along the way. A small script then exports the marts to the static JSON files this page fetches, which get published to GitHub Pages.

How the metrics are calculated

Real-estate opportunity score

Per commune and property type, for the most recent DVF year, with at least 5 qualifying sales that year. Blends five factors (0–100 total), proportionally rescaled from a suggested six-factor weighting: price/m² below the national median, multi-year price CAGR, transaction liquidity (sales per capita), population growth, and local median income. Property tax was in the original suggestion but isn't wired into the score — see "What's not included" below. Full breakdown on the Real estate tab's own "How is this calculated?" panel.

Price CAGR

Compounds growth between the earliest and latest years with at least 5 qualifying sales for that commune/type — currently up to a 2021–2024 span, capped at ±15%/year. Shown as "–"/neutral rather than a number when there isn't enough reliable history.

Population growth

Compound annual growth rate in official municipal population, 2017–2021 (from a different, INSEE-derived source than the current-year population figure used elsewhere), capped at ±5%/year. A shrinking population pulls the score down just as much as a growing one pulls it up — it's not a one-way bonus.

Transaction liquidity

This year's qualifying sales for that property type, divided by the commune's population — a rough proxy for how easy a property might be to resell, capped at 2% for full marks. It's an approximation (population isn't housing-unit count), not a precise turnover rate.

Département map

Median opportunity score across the communes currently shown by your filters — recomputed in the browser, not a fixed snapshot, so it always matches the rest of the page.

PEA return, risk & drawdown

Charts and stats rank by annualized return, not raw period return — period return isn't comparable across tickers with different amounts of ingested history, and pairing it against already-annualized volatility would mismatch units. Period return itself is a median of the first/last 5 trading days' price, not a single point-to-point comparison, so one glitchy data point can't swing it alone (a real fix: GLE.PA briefly showed +327% from that exact failure mode before it). Every figure uses adjusted close throughout (dividends and splits already priced in), and return/volatility/drawdown/Sharpe/vs.-benchmark are null until a ticker has 60+ trading days of history — short-history extrapolation goes wrong in both directions (an absurdly large annualized return from a noisy few-day average, or a falsely-flattering near-zero drawdown from not having lived through a bad week yet), so “not enough history” shows as “–”, never a fabricated number. Per-ticker Since/Until columns in the table make the actual ingested period visible. Vs. CW8.PA (simple excess return, not Beta-adjusted alpha) is computed over a date-aligned common window with the benchmark, since this watchlist now spans 5 exchanges with different holiday calendars. Country is derived from each ticker's ISIN prefix (legal domicile) rather than hand-typed — that caught 5 real mistakes (ArcelorMittal/Eurofins are Luxembourg, Airbus/Stellantis/ STMicroelectronics are Netherlands, despite all five being reputationally "French" CAC 40 names). Dividends are summed over the ingested history; dividend yield divides that by the last observed price. Full breakdown on the PEA tab's own "How is this calculated?" panel.

Wealth simulator

Client-side only — slider values never leave the browser, only aggregate market-assumption JSON is fetched. Every euro fills Livret A then LDDS first (safety-first), then splits between a preservation tier (Assurance Vie fonds euro, SCPI-in-AV, CAT) and a growth tier (PEA, leveraged real estate) by risk tolerance; PEA overflow past its real €150,000 cap (cumulative contributions, tracked separately from growth) falls back into the preservation tier. PEA growth uses a fixed ~7%/year long-run assumption, not the PEA tab's own live-computed returns - even the diversified benchmark ETF's 2-year figure was too high (17.9%/year) to compound realistically over a 20-40 year horizon. Most French capital-gains tax rules are applied once, to the final year's liquidation figure, not annually — CAT is the exception, taxed annually as its interest accrues (flat 30% PFU). Leveraged real estate needs a €15,000 minimum down payment to trigger at all; below that, the allocation goes to SCPI-in-AV instead. Illustrative starting heuristic, not investment advice — see the tab's own "How is this calculated?" panel for every assumption and simplification.

What's not included in the score (yet)

The opportunity score started from a broader six-factor idea — price, price appreciation, liquidity, population growth, income, and property tax/DPE. Five of those six now genuinely drive the score. Population growth in particular took two attempts: the first (INSEE's own bulk API) was too slow/unreliable to fetch from this environment (repeated stalls over ~20 minutes); the fix was a community-republished dataset (data.gouv.fr org "icem7") hosted on the same fast infrastructure DVF uses, covering 2017–2021 — not perfectly current, but a real multi-year trend rather than a same-year snapshot. Property tax rate (DGFiP, yearly) is ingested and shown in the Real estate table; it isn't a full sixth weighted factor yet — a genuinely new factor changes the weighting of everything else, so that's still a pending decision, not an oversight — but it's no longer purely informational either: egregious outliers (above 35%, ramping to a max 15-point deduction at 70%) now cost points as a guard rail (see the Real estate tab's own methodology panel for the exact formula). % of department population is also shown as a separate informational column: a size/concentration snapshot, distinct from the population growth rate that now scores. DPE (energy rating) distribution isn't ingested at all — it would need a new ADEME source and its own Airflow/dbt pipeline, a natural next addition, not ruled out.

Known limitations

This is a research aid built for learning, not investment advice. The real-estate score weights are hand-picked and meant to be tuned, not authoritative; PEA watchlist constituents (CAC 40, non-French EU blue chips, ETFs) drift over time and the list is a snapshot; and even after the data-quality filters, communes with only a handful of sales can still show noisier numbers than well-traded ones — the transaction count is shown everywhere precisely so you can judge that yourself.
